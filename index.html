Five Days in Penang

A travel diary from George Town and beyond

12 to 16 September 2026

Day by day

Day 1: Arriving in George Town

We dropped our bags at the guesthouse and walked straight into the old town. Every corner had a shophouse in a different colour, and the street art kept appearing where we least expected it. We got lost twice, which turned out to be the best way to see the lanes. Dinner was a plate of char kuey teow eaten standing up.

Day 2: Up Penang Hill

The funicular tram carried us up through the trees, and the air got cooler with every minute. The view from the top covered the whole island and the bridge to the mainland. Then came the misadventure: we nearly boarded the wrong tram on the way down and had to run back along the platform, laughing the whole way.

Day 3: A day of eating

We planned nothing except meals. Breakfast was kaya toast with soft eggs, lunch was assam laksa with its sharp tamarind broth, and dinner was a hawker centre crawl where we shared everything. Each stall had a queue, and every queue was worth it. I kept notes on each dish so I could rank them later.

Day 4: Hills and sea air

We took the bus out toward the north coast and spent the afternoon by the water. The sea was warm and the sand was busy but friendly. We watched the sun go down from the shore while eating roasted corn from a cart. It was the slowest day of the trip, and the one I remember best.

Day 5: Clan jetties and goodbye

On the last morning we walked out along the clan jetties, where whole families live in wooden houses above the water. The planks creaked under our feet and the tide moved quietly below. We bought a few small gifts, had one more coffee, and caught the afternoon ride home already planning the next visit.

Tips for next time

  • Carry small cash notes, because many hawker stalls do not take cards.
  • Walk the old town in the early morning, before the heat and the crowds.
  • Check the tram timetable on Penang Hill before heading back down.
  • Eat at the stalls with the longest local queues.
  • Bring a light rain jacket, since showers arrive without warning.
